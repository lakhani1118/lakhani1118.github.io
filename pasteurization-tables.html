Reading the tables

  • Hold times start once the center of the food reaches the listed temperature. They don’t include the time it takes to get there.
  • “Instant” means the food is pasteurized as soon as the center reaches that temperature.
  • Times are shown in hours, minutes and seconds, converted from the original decimal minutes.
  • Beef, lamb and pork times are for a 7D (10,000,000 : 1) reduction in Salmonella. That’s stricter than the FDA Food Code, which uses a 6.5D table, so some times are a little longer than in Baldwin’s Table C.1.
  • Chicken and turkey times are for a 7D (10,000,000 : 1) reduction in Salmonella, averaged by fat content: each low-fat time is the average of the source’s 1, 3 and 5% fat columns, and each high-fat time the average of its 7, 9 and 12% columns.

Source

Douglas Baldwin, A Practical Guide to Sous Vide Cooking, version 0.4k, Appendix C, “Government Pasteurization Tables”. The guide also covers heating times and the reasoning behind these numbers. The times on this page come from:

  • Beef, lamb, pork: FSIS. Time-temperature tables for cooking ready-to-eat poultry products. Notice 16‑05, Food Safety and Inspection Service, 2005. Attachment 1, 7‑log10 column. Baldwin’s Table C.1 uses the 6.5‑log10 times from the FDA Food Code (2009, section 3‑401.11.B.2).
  • Chicken, turkey (Table C.2): FSIS. Time-temperature tables for cooking ready-to-eat poultry products. Notice 16‑05, Food Safety and Inspection Service, 2005. Attachment 2.
